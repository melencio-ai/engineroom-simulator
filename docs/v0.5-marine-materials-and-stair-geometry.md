# ENGINE WATCH 3D v0.5 — Marine Materials and Inclined Stair Architecture

Status: implemented first-pass **PSP procedural environment**, 2026-11-18.
Derived from the user's merchant-vessel engine-room experience and a composite arrangement—not a specific vessel's approved GA, shipyard specification, or manufacturer manual.

## Source-backed construction direction

- **Painted dark-green steel checker plate** (raised-pattern anti-slip steel) for walkways, pump flats and machinery platforms. Selected engine-side areas use open/serrated steel grating instead. The term "corrugated" is not interchangeable with tread-pattern plate: true corrugated sheet is a different steel product.
- Structural materials: light warm off-white protected bulkheads; grey-green brackets, stiffeners and girders; green anti-slip flooring; pale painted handrails; restrained yellow at exposed hazards. Deck panel joints and tread relief should be visible.
- Main circulation uses **inclined steel stair-ladders**, not vertical hand ladders. Inclined egress stairs outside protected enclosures are subject to ship- and arrangement-specific SOLAS/IACS requirements. IACS UI SC277 Rev.1 (2024) gives a 60-degree maximum and 600 mm minimum clear width for applicable inclined escape stairs; these are regulatory **references**, not a certification claim for this game.
- Machinery foundations should visibly support load and align with main ship frames. Catwalks are partial flats around an uninterrupted central machinery well, not full floor slabs cutting through the engine.
- Most pipes should appear painted, metallic or insulated. Use narrow identification bands rather than painting every continuous pipe line in a saturated service colour. Reproduce actual identification with reference to ISO 14726 and the chosen vessel's piping diagrams before claiming compliance.
- Bright practical luminaire fixtures on each level and warmer ECR lighting, with deeper shadows only in recessed areas. Localized wear rather than pervasive rust.

References for later vessel-specific validation:
1. IACS UI SC277 Rev.1 (2024): https://www.classnk.com/hp/pdf/info_service/iacs_ur_and_ui/ui_sc277_rev.1_may_2024_cln.pdf
2. Lloyd's Register, 06/2025 *Engine Room Means of Escape Arrangements*: https://www.lr.org/en/knowledge/class-news/06-25/
3. IMO SOLAS Chapter II-2 machinery-space escape routes, including MSC.1/Circ.1511/Rev.1 and MSC.1/Circ.1689/Rev.1 (2026).
4. ISO 14726 *Ships and marine technology — Identification colours for the content of piping systems*: https://www.iso.org/standard/44744.html

- L3: auxiliary generator platform; stairways and auxiliary equipment remain separated by clear corridors.
- L2: main engine working platform with local instrumentation.
- L1: green pump flat with LO systems, with standby pump shifted away from the new stair entrance.
- L0: tank top, bedplate, recessed bilge covers and shaft direction.
- Service stair: forward side, near X -1.82 to +0.86, Z +5.0. Descend toward +X, climb toward -X. Use the crosswalk parallel at Z around +3.94 to return to the next flight entrance.
- Alternate stair: aft access bay X +13.5, Z -4.54 to -1.88. Descend toward positive Z, climb toward negative Z.
- Controls: analog/D-pad walking **directly on the sloped stairs**; L/R camera pitch; X reserved for machinery stations.

## PSP performance / accuracy caveats

- Environment uses vertex colours and geometry; raised chequer marks are an intentionally **coarse stylization** at 480x272, not photoreal materials.
- Repeating physical treads and geometry have a memory and vertex cost. CI checks a maximum vertex budget, but real PSP frame rate must still be profiled.
- Physical stair collision tracks a continuous incline (not each tread individually), avoiding camera shake. Side handrails and stairs are visual; the collision envelope is approximate and should be refined against player feedback.
- 3D equipment is mostly scenery; LO pump process simulation remains separate and unaffected. No real shipboard procedure is being taught or certified.
- Machinery-platform paint, clearance, escape construction, angle, step spacing and tread sizes require an approved ship-specific GA or engineering drawing before any authoritative training use.
- This **v0.5 implementation is not a certified escape-stair design**, and includes simplified visual representations of safety systems.
